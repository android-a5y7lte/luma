# ADR-056: RCS in Luma Messages, opt-in, through Google Messages

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-23
- **Owner:** Project Luma
- **Review basis:** the GrapheneOS PoC thread (grapheneos.social 117655062915356333 and replies, 21–22 Nov 2026); GrapheneOS/Messaging#456 (Jibe research, 27 Oct–10 Nov 2026); GrapheneOS `platform_packages_apps_GmsCompat` at dfa825dc (MIT); grapheneos.org `usage.html#rcs`; microg/GmsCore#2994 and the open PRs #3359/#3360; mautrix/gmessages `pkg/libgm` (AGPL-3.0); Luma `luma-messages-bridges/gmessages` (libgm e6cc2997, ADR-023); `docs/design/android-first-class.md`; `config/android/*`; FP6 native-RCS feasibility (RCC.14 against AT&T, Aug 2026). 
- **Extends:** ADR-051 (Luma Messages), ADR-023 (network helpers), ADR-050 (Android and other formats run as first-class apps), ADR-052 (one app model)
- **Revisit trigger:** Google publishes an RCS client API for third-party apps; microG merges Constellation and Asterism; GrapheneOS publishes its RCS source; a target carrier's own ACS starts provisioning Luma's client; Google changes Messages for web pairing or blocks uncertified devices from RCS.

## User problem

In the US and Canada, RCS is the default way phones talk to each other. Someone on a Luma phone who texts an iPhone or Android friend gets SMS/MMS today: no typing, no receipts, low-quality photos, broken group chats, and no encryption. They need RCS with end-to-end encryption to iPhone and Google Messages users, in the same Luma Messages inbox as everything else, without having to understand Jibe, IMS or Play services.

Our own RCS attempt (Aug 2026) worked as a client: AT&T's ACS accepted Luma's honest RCC.14 request. It answered `version=-1`, though: the line isn't entitled. AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon have all handed Android RCS to Google Jibe, and Jibe only provisions clients that come with Google's phone-number verification. GrapheneOS has now shown that a non-Google RCS app can reach Jibe, with MLS E2EE to iPhone and Google Messages, as long as Google Play services is present.

## What GrapheneOS actually does

From their posts, the Messaging repo issue and GmsCompat:

- Their app is a full RCS client: SIP/MSRP to Jibe, plus GSMA RCS E2EE (MLS). It runs on sandboxed Play services but not on Google Messages. A team member says registration and plain text work and MLS is in progress. **The source is not public**, and they say it "can't work outside of GrapheneOS".
- Jibe provisioning needs a phone-number token from Play services. Constellation verifies the number by MT SMS, MO SMS, carrier ID or TS.43 EAP-AKA, and returns a JWT that is sent to `rcs-acs-<carrier>.jibe.google.com` as `rcs_msisdn_token`. It also needs a consent record from Asterism and DroidGuard attestation tokens. There are regular check-ins.
- Play services serves those APIs only to Google Messages' signing identity. GrapheneOS's member wrote: "we can spoof signatures of our own apps". **The "special OS support" is the OS presenting their app to Play services as Google Messages.**
- Separately, GmsCompat adds two things: an opt-in grant of `USE_ICC_AUTH_WITH_DEVICE_IDENTIFIER` (an AOSP `signature|appop` permission) to Play services, so TS.43 carriers can run EAP-AKA against the SIM; and a checklist (Owner user, phone and network permissions, SMS role).

**Recommendation: B now. Keep D for carriers that allow it. Watch C. Don't build A.**

B delivers RCS with E2EE to iPhone and Google Messages now, and the dependence on Google matches GrapheneOS's ("depends on sandboxed Google Play"). It doesn't impersonate any app or device. Google's code does the RCS and the MLS, so iPhone and Google Messages interop is Google's problem, not ours. It also reuses a helper that Luma Messages already ships. C becomes the preferred backend if microG merges Constellation and Asterism and they pass on our lines, because it removes the second Android instance and real Play. A goes back on the table only if Google offers a supported API, or GrapheneOS publishes a client that doesn't need spoofing (they name a standalone mode for carriers that allow it, which is D).

## Decision

### 1. RCS is a transport inside Luma Messages

A conversation's transports are Luma (ADR-051), RCS and SMS/MMS. Luma Messages chooses per conversation and per send:

1. If both people are on Luma: Luma.
2. Else, if RCS is on and the helper reports the conversation as RCS: RCS through Google Messages.
3. Else: SMS/MMS through the host's own modem service (`ModemMessagingTransport`). This is unchanged.

If an RCS send fails or isn't confirmed within `UNCONFIRMED_SECONDS`, the message is marked, and "Send as text message" is offered. A message is never re-sent silently on a second transport, because of the existing duplicate-send kill switch. The thread shows which transport carried each message, and whether it was end-to-end encrypted, as Google Messages reports it (lock or no lock).

### 2. "Turn on RCS": off by default, nothing installed before the tap

The entry point is in Luma Messages, on a conversation with a non-Luma phone number ("Get typing, read receipts and encrypted chats with iPhone and Android. Turn on RCS") and in Accounts. Before anything is fetched, one sheet explains:

- RCS on most US carriers is run by Google. Turning it on installs Google Play services and Google Messages in a separate Android space used only for RCS, and asks you to sign in to Google once.
- Google will verify your phone number, which can involve a text message sent from your phone. Google sees your number, who you message and when, and the content of chats that aren't end-to-end encrypted. Chats with iPhone and Google Messages users that show the lock are encrypted end to end.
- About how much storage it takes and what it does to battery.
- You can turn it off at any time. That removes everything and gives your number back to plain texts.

Buttons: **Turn On RCS** / **Not Now**. No dark patterns, no nagging: after Not Now, the prompt comes back only from Accounts. This is an exception to "sensible defaults" because it installs a third party's proprietary stack and sends the number to Google; the explanation above is the reason.

After the tap: the RCS instance is created (§3), Play services, Play Store and Google Messages are fetched on the device (§7), the Google sign-in runs in that instance, Google Messages becomes the default SMS app inside that instance only, the helper pairs (§5), and the Accounts row shows progress: "Verifying your number… this can take up to 30 minutes".

### 3. The RCS instance, and microG

Real Play services and microG can't share an Android instance. Both use the package `com.google.android.gms`, and package identity is per instance, not per Android user. So:

- The main Android instance keeps microG, unchanged.
- RCS gets a **second, dedicated Android instance** ("RCS instance"): the same Luma Android image and version, built without microG and without the microG signature hook, with Play services installed as a privileged app *inside that instance*. The sandbox is the instance: it has no home share, contacts, calendar, location, microphone or camera (all `share_*=false`). Its only host paths are the network, the telephony presentation (§4) and notification delivery (Google Messages' notifications are turned off, because Luma Messages notifies).
- It is never shown in the app grid, it runs only while RCS is on, and it is per Luma user.
- Later, if route C qualifies, the RCS instance goes away and Google Messages moves into the main instance on microG.

GrapheneOS-style app-level sandboxing (gmscompat, MIT) in our main instance is not chosen. It still can't coexist with microG in one instance, and porting gmscompat's framework hooks to Lineage 23.2 is a large job with no benefit over a separate container.

### 4. Telephony presentation (new, shared prerequisite for B, C and A)

Waydroid has no radio, and Luma deliberately keeps the modem native (android-first-class: "do not hand Android ownership of the modem"). Google Messages refuses RCS without a SIM, and Constellation needs the subscriber. So the RCS instance gets a minimal `IRadio` AIDL service in its vendor image, backed by a host broker (`luma-telephony-presenter`) over ModemManager, plus imsd for SIM authentication. It presents:

- one subscription: MCC/MNC, SPN, carrier ID, MSISDN where known, SIM state, registration and data state, roaming;
- **no IMEI, serial or MEID.** Those calls return empty, as GmsCompat does with `getImei`, `getSimSerialNumber` and others. Nothing is written to the modem;
- **SIM authentication** (`getIccAuthentication`, EAP-AKA) for TS.43 carriers, granted to Play services only when RCS is on, and logged. FP6: through imsd's existing ISIM AKA. Pixel 9 and Titan: needs their modem path; until then, TS.43-only carriers are reported as not supported on that device;
- **SMS:** the host stays the owner of SMS/MMS. Incoming SMS go to Luma as today. Only messages that match Constellation's verification pattern (sender and body format, during an active verification window of at most 30 minutes) are also delivered to the RCS instance. Outgoing SMS from the RCS instance are allowed only to the verification destination during that window, and each one is shown to the user as "Google is verifying your number by text". Google Messages' own SMS fallback is refused, and Luma Messages offers "Send as text message" itself (§1);
- no voice and no IMS.

### 5. Pairing on the same device

The existing `gmessages` helper pairs today with Google-account cookies. Captured through a temporary Firefox profile, that's a second Google sign-in. For the RCS instance, add QR pairing (libgm `StartLogin`) and give the QR to Google Messages' scanner through the host camera bridge as a virtual camera frame (it already exists: 20261120-android-host-camera). That makes setup a single Google sign-in. If that proves unreliable, fall back to the cookie method. The pairing is labelled "This phone (RCS)" and can't be removed from Accounts on its own, only with Turn Off RCS.

### 6. Encryption and interoperability

- Google Messages does GSMA RCS E2EE (MLS) with Google Messages and with iOS. Luma does no RCS crypto of its own. The lock comes from Google Messages' per-message encryption state through libgm, and is never inferred by Luma.
- The Messages for web hop: the phone and the web client encrypt payloads with keys from the pairing; Google relays the ciphertext. Plaintext exists only in Google Messages inside the RCS instance and in Luma Messages' local store, both on the device.
- Luma-to-Luma conversations always prefer ADR-051's network, even when both people also have RCS.

### 7. Getting Google's binaries

Luma never redistributes or mirrors Play services, Play Store or Google Messages. They are fetched from Google on the device, only after the opt-in in §2 and the user's own Google sign-in in the RCS instance. Nothing passes through Luma's servers, and the Luma image carries no Google binary.

Open: the Play Store can't install or update Play services until a Play services build is already present, and Google publishes no standalone download of Play services for third-party systems. The bootstrap source has to be Google-hosted and licensed for this use before the feature ships. Google Play's own delivery to a signed-in device is the target. Third-party APK mirrors are excluded.

### 8. Devices and carriers

| Device | SIM authentication for TS.43 | Expected |
|---|---|---|
| FP6 | imsd ISIM AKA (works on AT&T) | First target: an AT&T-network line, Jibe |
| Pixel 9, Pixel 9 Pro Fold | not yet (Shannon modem path) | MT/MO-SMS verification carriers |
| Titan 2 Elite | not yet (MediaTek) | MT/MO-SMS verification carriers |
| Desktop, TV, no SIM | n/a | RCS is not offered. The row isn't shown |

- **Jibe carriers** (AT&T, T-Mobile, Verizon and most US/CA MVNOs, plus regions where Google provides RCS as the default provider): route B.
- **Native-carrier RCS** (operators whose own ACS provisions a standards client): keep the route D code. When the carrier's ACS returns a real configuration for Luma's honest client, D is used and Google isn't needed. The carrier probe runs once on SIM change. It reuses the Aug 2026 RCC.14 request and stops for good on `version=-1`.
- Unknown or unsupported carriers: the "Turn on RCS" row says "RCS isn't available with your carrier on Luma yet" and installs nothing. The probe for that is Google's own Jibe ACS lookup, run only after opt-in.

### 9. Failure behaviour

| Situation | What the person sees | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Verification taking a while | "Verifying your number… up to 30 minutes" | SMS keeps working; no RCS sends yet |
| Verification failed, or carrier not supported | "Google couldn't turn on RCS for this number" plus the reason in plain words; **Try Again** / **Turn Off RCS** | The instance stays installed until they choose, so a retry doesn't re-download |
| Device refused (integrity or uncertified) | "Google doesn't offer RCS on this device right now" | Offers Turn Off RCS. We don't work around it with a fake identity |
| RCS instance or helper down | Accounts row "RCS paused", plus a thread banner | Outgoing messages offer "Send as text message". The instance is restarted under the existing Android watchdog |
| Pairing lost (Google unpaired the web session) | "Reconnect RCS" | Re-pairs through §5 without another sign-in where possible |
| Turn Off RCS | Confirmation saying that texts from RCS users go back to SMS | First deregisters the number from Google's RCS (Google Messages "Turn off chat features", then Google's deregistration page if the instance is broken), then unpairs, then deletes the instance and its data. Otherwise RCS senders keep sending into a dead registration |
| SIM change | The row resets | Re-verification on the new SIM, only if RCS is still on |

### 10. Power

- Budget: the RCS instance adds at most **2% battery per day** on the FP6 while idle (screen off, no RCS conversation activity), measured against the same day with RCS off.
- The instance is suspended (frozen through Waydroid's lifecycle API) whenever the host has no RCS conversation activity. It is thawed when Luma Messages is in front, when an RCS thread is open or a send is queued, and for a short scheduled sync window (default every 15 minutes, aligned with the host's other maintenance wakeups). Between windows, incoming RCS waits in Jibe's store-and-forward and arrives on the next thaw; the sender sees "Sent", not "Delivered", until then.
- The thaw interval is the tuning knob: the shortest interval that stays inside the budget is chosen from measurement, per device.
- The libgm long-poll runs only while the instance is thawed.

### 11. Not decided here

- Group MMS versus RCS groups across transports.
- Bridging Google Messages from a separate Android phone (today's Accounts feature) when this device also has RCS on. Proposed: one Google Messages account row per phone, with this device's own row preferred for its number.

## Verification before shipping

1. Runtime gate: the RCS instance reaches Play Integrity `MEETS_BASIC_INTEGRITY` under Luma's own build identity. If it doesn't, route B stops here; we don't spoof identity to pass.
2. Pairing: libgm pairs with Google Messages in the same device's RCS instance.
3. FP6 with a real SIM: verification policy recorded, registration reaches Connected, E2EE (lock) both ways with Google Messages and iOS, fallback to SMS without duplicate sends.
4. Turn Off: the number is deregistered and RCS senders reach it by SMS again.
5. Power: §10 budget met on the FP6.

## Consequences

- Luma's first proprietary Google component on the device, isolated in its own instance and present only after an explicit opt-in.
- A telephony presentation layer in Android that later also serves visual voicemail, carrier apps and route C.
- Luma Messages depends on libgm for RCS. We already carry that dependency (ADR-023); a libgm break now affects RCS too. Keep the helper pinned, and ship libgm updates on the Depot first-party fast path.
- If Google requires a certified device for RCS, route B stops working on Luma. No workaround that fakes identity is acceptable, so the answer then is C or D.
